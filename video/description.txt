Half a Page. What AI scrapers silently drop.

When an AI agent reads a website, a scraper turns the page into text first, and whatever the scraper misses, the agent never sees. I built WebFidelity, a benchmark with planted-fact test pages and 74 real sites, and pointed it at Firecrawl. By default, Firecrawl snapshots a page about 300 ms after it loads, doesn't wait for in-flight requests, and runs in a 100,000-pixel-tall window that can't scroll. A free setting (actions) lifts recall from 52% to 89%, and on ordinary real pages Firecrawl does well (85% across 46 held-out sites).

The claim: scrapers can lose content without any sign in the output, and you can only catch it by measuring against an answer key, on data you never tuned on.
